Every halving/doubling of frequency beyond cutoff costs a fixed number of decibels: 6 per pole. One pole = 6 dB/oct, a biquad = 12, cascades give 18 and 24. The audition uses the same filter sections represented by the response curve, so the sound and graph stay matched.

Q is how hard the filter rings at cutoff. Above Q ≈ 0.707 a lowpass develops a resonant bump right at fc — the "synth filter" whistle. Push Q on live noise and the cutoff turns into a tone; pull it back and the bump melts into a gentle knee.

A crossover splits one signal into a LPF and an HPF at the same fc. Magnitudes alone are not enough: the two outputs keep their phase, and their summed response depends on the filter topology. In this lab, 6/12/18 dB use Butterworth sections; 24 dB uses a Linkwitz-Riley 4th-order pair. The dark curve is the complex sum of the same responses used for the audition. Listen to SUM to hear the phase relationship at the crossover.
