panning laws · pan vs balance · M-S stereo width & mono compatibility

A panner decides how much of the signal goes left vs right. A linear law (L = 1−p, R = p) keeps the channel-amplitude sum constant, but the centre position is 3 dB lower in summed stereo power than the hard-left/right positions — the image "dips" as it passes through the middle. A constant-power law (L = cos θ, R = sin θ) keeps radiated power steady so the centre doesn't dip — but the mono sum then rises +3 dB at centre. Sweep the pan and watch the per-channel curves and the two sum readouts.

Pan places a (summed) source somewhere new — it happily collapses a stereo signal to mono first. Balance only tilts an existing stereo image by attenuating the far side, preserving the original width. Feed it the stereo source and A/B the two: pan turns two performers into one walking performer; balance keeps both and just leans.

Mid/Side math: M = (L+R)/2, S = (L−R)/2. Rebuilding with L′ = M + w·S, R′ = M − w·S gives a width knob — w = 0 is mono, 1 is untouched, >1 is "wide". The catch: everything you widen lives in S, and S vanishes in mono (and correlates negatively). Watch the goniometer open horizontally as you widen, watch correlation fall, then flip the monitor to mono and hear the widened layer disappear.
