Sound squeezes through openings and curls around edges. How much it spreads is set by wavelength vs. opening size: long λ (low notes) fan out almost fully; short λ (high notes) beam straight through, leaving a shadow zone. That's why you hear the bass around a corner but not the hi-hats.

Materials eat sound. The absorption coefficient α is the fraction of energy a surface absorbs at a given frequency — the rest reflects. Soft porous materials soak up highs; thin panels trap lows; an open window absorbs everything. Notice how carpet is a high-frequency absorber but barely touches bass.
