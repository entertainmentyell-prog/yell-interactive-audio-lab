Every synth sound starts with an oscillator. The four classic shapes are really just different harmonic recipes: a sine has one partial, saw adds every harmonic at 1/n (−6 dB/oct), square keeps the odd ones at 1/n, triangle keeps odd ones at 1/n² (−12 dB/oct) — which is why it sounds so much softer than a saw.

The analog workhorse: a harmonically rich oscillator (saw/square) pushed through a resonant lowpass. The filter doesn't add anything — it sculpts by removing harmonics above cutoff, and Q makes the ones at the edge ring. Sweep cutoff and hear the classic "wah".

Pitch is only half the instrument — the contour tells you what it is. A fast attack reads as plucked/struck; a slow attack as bowed or pad-like. Trigger a note and watch the amp envelope: Attack to peak, Decay to Sustain level, held, then the Release tail after note-off.

Frequency modulation: a modulator oscillator wiggles the carrier's frequency. The spectrum doesn't just tilt — it grows sidebands at carrier ± k·fm with amplitudes given by Bessel functions Jₖ(I). Integer ratios give harmonic, musical tones; the index controls how bright.
